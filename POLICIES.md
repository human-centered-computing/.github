# Human-Centered Computing Policies

These policies apply to every project, repository, research activity, dataset, model, document, and community contribution developed under the Human-Centered Computing organization.

## 1. Human Benefit

All work must aim to create a meaningful benefit for people, communities, society, or the natural environment.

Technology should strengthen human dignity, agency, safety, accessibility, creativity, learning, resilience, and well-being.

Projects designed primarily to manipulate, exploit, discriminate against, deceive, unlawfully surveil, or harm people or communities are outside the scope of this organization.

## 2. Human Oversight

Artificial intelligence should support human capabilities rather than remove meaningful human control.

Projects involving consequential decisions should:

* keep people appropriately informed,
* provide meaningful human oversight,
* communicate uncertainty and limitations,
* support review and correction,
* and avoid presenting uncertain outputs as established facts.

## 3. Open Source by Default

Software, models, documentation, evaluation methods, and reusable research artifacts should be published under appropriate open-source or open-content licenses.

Restrictions may be applied only when disclosure would conflict with:

* personal privacy,
* information security,
* research ethics,
* intellectual property obligations,
* contractual or legal requirements,
* third-party licensing conditions,
* responsible vulnerability disclosure,
* or human and community safety.

Any temporary restriction should be documented and reviewed periodically.

## 4. Open Science and Reproducibility

Academic and technical claims should be supported by transparent methods and reproducible evidence whenever possible.

Projects should publish, where legally and ethically permitted:

* research questions and hypotheses,
* methodology,
* experimental configurations,
* evaluation criteria,
* source code,
* dependency and model information,
* known limitations,
* and reproduction instructions.

Negative results and material limitations must not be concealed.

## 5. Privacy and Data Protection

Projects must collect and process only the data required for a legitimate purpose.

Personal, sensitive, biometric, medical, legal, educational, financial, or location data must not be published without an appropriate legal and ethical basis.

Datasets should document provenance, consent conditions, intended uses, known limitations, access restrictions, and applicable anonymization or de-identification procedures.

## 6. Safety and Security

Projects must consider foreseeable misuse, security vulnerabilities, and unintended consequences.

Security vulnerabilities should be reported through responsible disclosure channels when public disclosure could create immediate harm.

Security-sensitive capabilities may require additional review, staged publication, access controls, or limited release.

## 7. Fairness, Accessibility, and Inclusion

Projects should consider:

* accessibility,
* cultural and linguistic diversity,
* demographic representation,
* algorithmic bias,
* unequal access to technology,
* and the needs of vulnerable or underserved communities.

Systems must not claim universal validity when they have only been evaluated in limited populations, languages, regions, or environments.

## 8. Transparency

Every mature project should clearly disclose:

* its intended purpose,
* its development status,
* its maintainers,
* its license,
* its dependencies,
* its known limitations,
* relevant risks,
* and whether artificial intelligence is used.

Project documentation must distinguish completed capabilities from planned, experimental, or proposed capabilities.

## 9. Academic Integrity

Fabricated results, manipulated evaluations, plagiarism, misleading comparisons, concealed conflicts of interest, and unverifiable claims are prohibited.

Authorship, attribution, and contribution credit must reflect actual intellectual and technical contributions.

## 10. Multilingual and Universal Design

English is the canonical language for source code, technical documentation, and international collaboration.

Projects should be multilingual by design where practical and should support structured localization rather than hard-coded language dependencies.

Projects may begin with local needs but should be designed for adaptation across different countries, cultures, institutions, and sectors.

## 11. Sustainable Engineering

Projects should consider computational efficiency, energy consumption, maintainability, deployment cost, environmental impact, and long-term accessibility.

A larger or more computationally expensive solution should not automatically be preferred when a smaller and more efficient approach can meet the real-world need.

## 12. Community Conduct

Participation must remain respectful, evidence-based, constructive, and inclusive.

Disagreement is welcome when it focuses on ideas, methods, evidence, design decisions, and project outcomes.

Harassment, discrimination, threats, personal attacks, and deliberate disruption are not acceptable.

## 13. Licensing

Every public software repository must include an approved open-source license.

Datasets, documentation, academic content, model weights, and media should use licenses appropriate to each artifact type.

Third-party components must be used in compliance with their original licenses and attribution requirements.

## 14. Project Admission

A project may be hosted by Human-Centered Computing when it:

* has a clear human or societal purpose,
* aligns with human-centered transformation principles,
* has an identifiable maintainer,
* includes sufficient documentation,
* follows ethical, legal, privacy, security, and licensing requirements,
* and has a credible path toward research, implementation, education, or real-world impact.

## 15. Project Review

Projects may be reviewed, archived, restricted, or removed when they:

* materially violate these policies,
* create unacceptable safety or legal risks,
* misrepresent results,
* fail to address serious security concerns,
* become abandoned without a transition plan,
* or no longer align with the organization’s mission.

Whenever possible, the reason for archival, restriction, or removal should be documented.

---

**Human benefit by purpose. Human oversight by design. Open source by default. Scientific evidence by principle.**
